Description (<=260 chars):
A small lab for every STEM field, one lane at a time. Each lane asks one exact question, computes inside a fixed bound, and checks the answer a second way. Failures stay visible. Mostly plain Python, no model calls, no background jobs. Free, MIT.

First comment (maker story):
Fieldbook is the map of science and math. Noema is the lab, one lane at a time, where each field gets an exact question and a check that can fail.

Two real examples from the repo. The math lane works through Collatz first descents and Goldbach. The physics lane counts exact 2D Ising energies and checks them against Kaufman's formula.

The rule is the point. State one question. Compute inside a fixed bound. Check it a second way, with code that shares nothing with the first. Keep failures visible. Say what was observed, and keep that apart from what was proved.

Most rows in the ledger reproduce known work. That's on purpose. An engine has to earn trust on answers we already have before anyone points it at something new.

There are no model calls, no background jobs and no automatic pushes. Most lanes use plain Python with the standard library. Biology needs Biopython.

It's free and MIT licensed. Questions about a lane, or a claim that seems too big, are welcome in the comments.
